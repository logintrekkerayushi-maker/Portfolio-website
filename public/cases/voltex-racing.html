Sector 1 Pit Lane
Business Context

A team with reach
but mispriced value

Voltex Racing is a fictional mid-grid Formula E team competing across the ABB FIA Formula E World Championship. With races in 12 cities, a projected 180 million cumulative broadcast audience, and a digital following of 1.2 million, the team has genuine commercial value to offer sponsors.

The problem was structural. Voltex was selling that value through a single flat-rate package at €450,000 per season. No tiers, no differentiation, no entry pathway. Tech companies, sustainability brands, and financial services firms with very different budgets and objectives were all being offered exactly the same thing.

Meanwhile, two comparable Formula E teams had moved to tiered structures and were converting mid-market sponsors at €120,000 to €280,000 — a segment Voltex wasn't approaching at all.

The core problem

A flat €450,000 package was acting as a filter, not a funnel. It excluded every sponsor whose budget sat below that threshold and gave no upsell path for those who wanted more involvement. The team had no answer for a €180,000 prospect and no retention mechanism for existing partners wanting to grow.

Personal perspective — EY background

"At EY, complex B2B value propositions had to be packaged for stakeholders with very different risk tolerances and budgets. Pricing a motorsport sponsorship is the same structural problem — translating brand exposure into commercial ROI that a CFO will sign off on. The methodology is identical."

Sector 2 The Hairpin
Competitive Teardown

"In sponsorship pricing, the worst outcome is not being too expensive. It is being un-tiered — invisible to the majority of your potential market."

Adapted from IEG Sponsorship Report, 2023

I benchmarked publicly available sponsorship data across four motorsport properties. The pattern was consistent across every successful commercial programme — tiered pricing with value-based anchor points. Voltex was the only outlier still operating on a flat rate.

The competitive gap is structural, not commercial. A prospect with €180k sees no entry point. A prospect with €800k sees no reason to pay more than €450k. Both ends of the market are being lost simultaneously.

Anchor pricing logic

The Official Partner tier at €220k sits at the Van Westendorp indifference point, driving maximum conversion volume. The Principal tier at €400k sits below the €450k exit threshold to create a psychologically accessible premium step. The Associate tier at €100k opens the pipeline to prospects currently being missed entirely by the flat-rate model.

Bottom line

The tier redesign does not require Voltex to win more races. It requires the commercial team to sell the same product in a structure that matches how sponsors actually make buying decisions. At the target scenario, modelled sponsorship revenue increases from an estimated €450k to approximately €3.08M — by converting the mid-market pipeline the flat-rate model was structurally unable to reach. These are scenario projections based on comparable team benchmarks and should be treated as directional.

Podium Cooldown Room
What I'd Do Next

If I had 6 more months
and a commercial mandate

The tier structure is the foundation. The next phase builds the sales and retention infrastructure around it.

01
Sponsor prospecting database
Build a scored prospect list of 60 to 80 companies across tech, fintech, and sustainability sectors. Prioritise by budget match to each tier, procurement timeline, and competitor sponsor status. Quality of pipeline matters more than volume at this stage.
02
Live brand tracking dashboard
Implement real-time exposure tracking showing sponsors their media value after each race. This removes the renewal objection of "we can't measure this" and creates a data-led retention conversation at the 6-month review point.
03
Tier upgrade pathway programme
At month 4, Associate partners receive a personalised report showing what they would have received at Official tier. This creates an evidence-based upsell conversation rather than a cold ask — turning renewal into a natural commercial discussion.
04
City-specific sponsorship add-ons
Formula E's city-race format is a unique asset no other series has. Design market-specific add-ons — e.g. a Singapore race package for an APAC-focused brand — letting sponsors buy into specific geographies at a premium to standard tier pricing.
