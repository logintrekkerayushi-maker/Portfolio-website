Independent Project 04 · Marketing Finance & ROI Strategy

The company that said it didn't need marketing just spent 47% more on it.

“I used Wise to send rent money to a landlord in Spain, and it made me curious: how does a company famous for never advertising end up increasing its marketing budget by nearly half in a single year?”

For over a decade, Wise's entire growth story was word-of-mouth, referrals, and content, not paid advertising. In FY2025, that changed: marketing spend rose 47% to £53.8 million. This project reconstructs the internal case that shift would have required, from a board that built its identity on not needing to pay for growth.

Stop 01 · Context

A decade of proving marketing wasn't necessary.

Verified, public record

Wise (formerly TransferWise) was founded in 2011 on a simple frustration: hidden fees and poor exchange rates in international transfers. Its early VP of Growth has said plainly that in the company's early years, “product economics did not support paid marketing.” Instead, Wise grew through referrals, transparency as a brand story, and a large content and SEO operation that drove tens of millions of organic visits. Word-of-mouth, tracked through Net Promoter Score, was treated internally as the primary growth engine, not a side effect of good service.

That identity became part of the company's public story: the fintech that didn't need to advertise because the product spoke for itself. Which makes the FY2025 number worth pausing on.

Stop 02 · The Diagnosis

Word-of-mouth has a ceiling. Wise was likely close to it.

Verified, public record

In FY2025, Wise's marketing spend rose 47% to £53.8 million, disclosed specifically as an investment “to drive incremental adoption and engagement.”

My reconstruction

Organic and referral growth scales with an existing customer base, but it has a structural limit: it only reaches people already adjacent to your current customers, friends, colleagues, people in the same online spaces. Reaching genuinely new segments (a different age group, a different country's banking habits, corporate or business customers rather than individuals) usually requires demand you go out and create, not demand that arrives on its own. The likely internal argument was not “word-of-mouth stopped working,” it was “word-of-mouth stopped being enough to reach the customers we haven't reached yet.”

Stop 03 · The Financial Model

Proving new spend without disowning fourteen years of philosophy.

My reconstruction

The hardest part of this pitch isn't the math, it's that it can't look like an admission the old strategy failed. The model has to prove paid spend is additive to word-of-mouth, not a replacement for it.

01
Segment the ceiling
Map which customer segments organic and referral channels are structurally unlikely to reach, new geographies, new demographics, business accounts, rather than treating “growth” as one number.
02
Test paid as a multiplier
Run paid campaigns specifically in markets or segments where referral density is already low, so any lift is additive rather than cannibalizing existing organic growth.
03
Keep NPS as the guardrail
Since word-of-mouth was always downstream of customer satisfaction, track NPS alongside the new spend. If paid acquisition brings in less satisfied customers, that's the signal to pull back before it damages the core engine.

Stop 04 · The Board-Ready Business Case

The pitch that extends the story instead of contradicting it.

My reconstruction

A board that has spent a decade telling investors “we don't need to buy growth” will not approve a pitch that sounds like a reversal. It will approve one that sounds like the next chapter of the same philosophy.

The Recommendation, As It Would Be Pitched
Increase paid marketing spend by up to 50%, targeted specifically at the segments referral and content growth structurally cannot reach. Success is measured not by total new customers, but by NPS holding steady or rising among customers acquired this way, proof that Wise found new customers without becoming a company that has to buy the ones it used to earn.

Stop 06 · What I'd Do Next

Where this reasoning would need to keep proving itself.

I
Track NPS by acquisition channel
Not just overall NPS, specifically whether paid-acquired customers refer others at the same rate as organic ones.
II
Watch for cannibalization
Confirm paid spend isn't simply capturing demand that referrals would have generated anyway in the same markets.
III
Set a reversal trigger
Decide in advance what NPS or referral-rate decline would justify scaling paid spend back down.
IV
Re-test the ceiling annually
The segments organic growth can't reach today may shrink or grow as the product and markets change.
